Wada you know about colour?

Every day, ten colours from Sanzo Wada’s Dictionary of Color Combinations, a Japanese colour book from the 1930s full of shades with names like Hermosa Pink, Cossack Green and Vinaceous Tawny. Can you put a name to each one? If you get stuck, every colour comes with a cryptic crossword clue, and a second hint that points you towards the answer.

Choose a name your friends will know you by. There’s nothing to sign up for: this phone or computer will remember you.

How do you want to answer?
Which round?
Each colour is worth 6 points if you name it without help, 3 with the clue and 1 with both hints. Type mode doubles everything.
How the cryptic clues work

Every clue has two halves. The definition, at the start or the end, describes the colour. The rest is wordplay that builds its name: anagrams (look for words like twisted or badly), words joined end to end, hidden words, or sound-alikes. Colour words have fixed stand-ins: red is Communist or flushed, blue is down or sad, green is naive or envious, yellow is cowardly or chicken, pink is blushing or in fine fettle, purple is royal. After each answer, you’ll see how the clue was put together.
