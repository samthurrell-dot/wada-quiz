Ridgway named his colours after whatever showed them best: a fruit, a stone, a bird, a city, a painter, a new dye. Every square below is one of Wada’s 159 colours. Tap any of them to see what it’s named after.

About the pictures and how sure this is

Origins are based on standard word histories and marked sure, likely or unsure. Each name is sorted by its most specific word (Olive Buff counts as olive). Orange and violet began as a fruit and a flower but are counted as plain colour words. Colour groups come from each colour’s measured hue, so a few land where you might not expect.

Colour check: each colour’s picture is chosen from several candidates by measuring, in your browser, how much of the picture is a close match to the Wada colour (using CIEDE2000, a standard colour-difference formula). A picture is only shown if at least 10% of it is a close match, and some were chosen by hand.

Pictures come from Wikimedia Commons, plus a few from other open libraries (Openverse and the Art Institute of Chicago) whose licence is shown with them. Wikimedia pictures marked “public domain by age” are paintings, prints and plates from 1909 or earlier. The rest are modern photos; their credit links show the photographer and licence. Each picture’s source page has its full credit and licence.
